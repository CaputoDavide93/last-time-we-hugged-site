Privacy policy

Last Time We Hugged doesn’t collect any data about you. Everything you write in the app stays on your phone unless you choose to send it somewhere.

Effective 6 October 2026. Applies to the Last Time We Hugged (LTWH) apps for iPhone, Apple Watch and Android, and to this website.

In short

  • No account. There is nothing to sign up for.
  • No servers. LTWH has no server, and the app never sends your journal to the developer.
  • No analytics, ads or tracking. No third-party SDKs for any of these.
  • Nothing to buy. The app is free, with no purchases and no ads.
  • No AI. Insights come from plain rules that run on your phone.
  • You decide what leaves the phone. Only when you sync with your partner, turn on iCloud sync, or send a file yourself.

What the app keeps, and where

Your couple profile (both first names, sex, character traits, when you got together, an optional nickname), the arguments you log (date, who raised it, topic, what sparked it, intensity, duration, how it ended, who apologised first, what you learned and what you’d do differently), your daily mood check-ins and your custom topics are kept inside the app’s private storage. The developer never receives them and can’t see them.

The app leaves this data out of iCloud and computer backups on iPhone, and turns off Google backup and phone-to-phone transfer on Android. That keeps the journal private, but it also means a lost or reset phone takes it with it, unless you exported a backup file yourself (see below) or turned on iCloud sync.

Your settings, such as which reminders are on and whether the app lock is on, are kept apart from the journal. On iPhone they sit in the phone’s standard app settings, which iPhone backups do include, along with the date of your last argument and your best streak, which the widget counts from. None of it is anything you wrote.

Permissions, all optional

Each permission is asked for only when you use the feature that needs it. Everything else in the app works without them.

  • Notifications: the app first explains what reminders do, on one short screen, and the phone asks only if you tap “Allow reminders”. That screen appears when you save an argument that’s still open (until you choose “Not now”) or when you turn on a reminder in Settings. Reminders remind you about an open argument a day later, mark streak milestones, end a pause, and, only if you switch them on, bring the Sunday check-in and the flashpoint heads-up. They never name what you argued about.
  • Face ID, fingerprint or passcode: only if you turn on the app lock. The phone checks it’s you; the app only learns “yes” or “no”.
  • Local network (iPhone): asked when you first start nearby sync, so your phone can find your partner’s on your Wi-Fi.

The app never asks for your location, contacts, calendar, microphone, camera or photo library.

Things you share into the app

Only what you type. The app has no share extension and no Siri or voice commands.

Widgets, Lock Screen and watch

  • The home-screen widget shows how many days since your last argument and your best streak. It never shows names, topics or anything you wrote.
  • Notifications on the Lock Screen never name what you argued about.
  • The Apple Watch app shows your streak and lets you quick-log an argument (who raised it, the topic and the intensity). That travels over Apple’s direct link between your watch and your iPhone, not through a server.

Syncing, off until you use it

  • Nearby sync: your phone and your partner’s swap journals directly over your own Wi-Fi, iPhone or Android. You pair with an 8-character code, and the journals are encrypted between the two phones. Nothing goes through a server.
  • iCloud sync (iPhone): when you switch it on, Apple keeps your journal in step across your own devices through your personal iCloud account. The developer can’t read it. Apple’s iCloud terms apply.

Backups and files you choose to send

  • Export backup: creates a file with your whole journal and hands it to the share sheet.
  • Monthly report and therapist PDF: a summary you can share. The PDF can leave out your personal notes (what sparked it, what you learned, what you’d do differently); dates, names, topics and the other details stay in.
  • Words: a repair phrase you edit and send from Messages or any app.

Where these go (Files, AirDrop, email, a messaging app) is your choice, and that service’s privacy terms apply.

Links

The privacy and contact links in Settings open only when you tap them, in your browser. The app never loads them itself.

Payments

None. The app is free, with nothing to buy, no subscriptions and no ads, so it contains no store billing code.

Sign-in

“Continue with Apple” on iPhone is optional and only fills in your first name during setup. Apple handles the sign-in on its own servers, under its privacy policy. The app keeps the name and Apple’s sign-in identifier on the phone and sends them nowhere.

The apps also contain an optional “Continue with Google” that works the same way, through Google’s sign-in library on Android. The current version doesn’t show it. If it is turned on in a later version, this page will say so first.

Deleting your data

Settings → Reset all data removes everything the app keeps on the phone: the journal, mood check-ins, custom topics, settings, any sign-in identifier, reminders, leftover export files and the widget’s streak. Deleting the app does the same. If iCloud sync is on, the reset reaches your iCloud copy too; if it’s off, remove an older copy in iPhone Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Storage. Files you exported are yours and stay where you put them.

Children

The app collects no personal information from anyone, including children. It’s meant for adults in a relationship.

This website

This site has no cookies, analytics or trackers. It is hosted on GitHub Pages; GitHub may keep standard server logs, such as IP addresses, under its privacy statement.

If you contact us

If you email for support, your address and message are used only to reply to you, and aren’t shared with anyone. Please don’t send anything from your journal unless it’s needed to explain a problem.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is posted here with a new date. Anything that would send data off your phone in a new way is explained in the app before it takes effect.
